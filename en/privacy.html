Last updated: 21 October 2026 · applies to the FREQ app (iOS and Android) and this website.

Who is responsible

Controller under the GDPR: Luka Veselinovic, Nordwestbahnstraße 91/39, 1200 Vienna, Austria — lukaves567@gmail.com.

The short version

  • FREQ does not sell data and shows no ads.
  • Your dot on the map is shown only to the members of your running convoy. Beyond that, your location goes to map, search, weather and price services when you use them — and with "friends nearby" as well as speed-camera and hazard reports, an entry sits in the database that other drivers can read. The details are in the table below.
  • In a convoy you also share your battery level (the others see a sign when it is nearly empty — you can turn this off) and hard braking: if you brake hard from 60 km/h or more, FREQ warns the members behind you. Both end with the convoy; braking events are not stored at all.
  • The radio is an open channel, not a push-to-talk key. Once you are on a convoy's radio, your microphone is open and the others hear you — even with the screen off. "Quiet" or muting ends that immediately. Outside a convoy the microphone is off.
  • Conversations are never recorded or stored — they pass live through the voice server and are gone afterwards.
  • The app never listens secretly and does not analyse anything you say.

Legal bases

Processing is based on the user agreement (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR — sign-in, convoy, radio, navigation), on legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f) — abuse prevention and operational security), and, where the app explicitly asks you, on your consent (Art. 6(1)(a); for crash reports additionally § 165(3) Austrian TKG 2021): location, microphone, crash reports and speed-camera warnings in countries requiring opt-in. You can withdraw any consent at any time in the settings.

For the new features: weather and weather warnings, closures, places and fuel-station details, fuel and charging prices, webcams, offline maps, "avoid motorways", the speed-limit preview, the break suggestion, planned rides and the battery level in a convoy serve the performance of the user agreement (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR) — they run because you use the feature. The hard-braking warning to the other members and the app identifier sent to OpenFreeMap are based on legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f)): the safety of the members behind you and the usage rules of the free map service. You can object at any time (Art. 21 GDPR, e-mail above); the hard-braking warning is only sent while you are on a convoy's radio anyway.

Reports and automatic suspension

If three different accounts report the same account within 30 days, it is suspended automatically and provisionally — without prior human review, for at most 7 days. You see the reason in the app and can object by e-mail at any time; the case is then reviewed by a human. Stored for this are the reporter's identifier, the reported account, the reason and the time.

Retention

A convoy's position data is deleted when the convoy ends; a server job cleans up orphaned convoy rooms. Your account and friends list remain until you delete your account (in the app settings or by e-mail). Crashlytics retains crash reports for 90 days.

For the new features: FREQ deletes your battery level in a convoy as soon as you leave the convoy; our server cleans up orphaned rooms. Hard-braking events and the route points for weather and weather warnings are not stored. Our server deletes planned rides 24 hours after departure, with their invitations and answers; the planner can delete them earlier at any time. Offline maps, the break timer and cached places (6 hours at most) stay only on your device.
